# Does the macro action space have headroom above the scripted baseline?
# Answer: no headroom is demonstrable, and the argument that there was is wrong.
#
# Run before spending GPU, on CPU, 4 pinned fresh floors, 15-min episodes.
# Strokes are hand-scheduled (cycle through N lanes, alternating sides) at
# far_to_near's own pose (standoff 0.400 m, tilt 0.550 rad), so this measures
# the SPACE, not a policy.

  lanes   spacing   clean fraction
     12    38.2 cm  0.305 +- 0.037
     15    30.0 cm  0.342 +- 0.043
     18    24.7 cm  0.284 +- 0.048
     24    18.3 cm  0.301 +- 0.050
     30    14.5 cm  0.347 +- 0.045

  far_to_near reference   0.333 +- 0.046   (same floors, same horizon)

NO TREND. Over a 2.6x range of lane spacing the result is 0.28-0.35, and every
value sits inside every other value's error bars. The best hand-built schedule
(0.347) beats far_to_near by 0.014 against a +-0.045 spread -- not a result.

A PREDICTION OF MINE THAT FAILED, recorded because it was the case for spending
GPU. I argued: 35 strokes x 17.7 cm effective swath x 7 m = 43 m^2 of a 58.8 m^2
bay = 74% coverage, which through the project's own regression
f_clean = 0.596*c + 0.025 gives ~0.47 clean, well above far_to_near's 0.333 at
~52% coverage. The 18-lane schedule's 0.284 was therefore blamed on 24.7 cm
spacing leaving gaps against a 17.7 cm swath.

Tested directly: 24 lanes at 18.3 cm, spacing matched to the swath, scores
0.301. The predicted 0.46 does not appear. So the regression does not
extrapolate the way I applied it -- it was fitted across ROUTING STRATEGIES
whose coverage differed for structural reasons (chase_worst at c=0.04 versus
full sweeps at c=0.94), and reusing it to predict the effect of re-spacing lanes
within one strategy is not a use it supports.

WHAT THIS DOES AND DOES NOT SAY

It does NOT say coverage stopped mattering. chase_worst still scores 0.059
against far_to_near's 0.333 by covering 4% of the floor, and that gap is the
largest in the project. Coverage dominates BETWEEN strategy classes.

It says that WITHIN the class "full passes wall to trough", how the passes are
spaced is not a lever. That is consistent: 35 strokes sweep a roughly fixed
area whatever their spacing, and spacing only trades gaps against overlap.

CONSEQUENCE FOR TRAINING. The gate for spending GPU on the macro space was a
hand-built schedule beating 0.333. The best of five is 0.347 +- 0.045, which is
the baseline to within noise. A learned policy searching this space has no
demonstrated headroom to find, so the GPU run was not launched. This does not
prove a policy cannot do better -- an adaptive policy can vary pose per stroke
and respond to where grit actually is, which no fixed schedule here does -- but
the evidence for headroom is now absent rather than merely unproven, and that
is the wrong side of the gate.

FOLLOW-UP: does NON-UNIFORM allocation beat uniform? No. (added after Andrew
asked whether the lawnmower pattern is provably optimal -- it is not, and the
test above had a real gap: every schedule in it allocated strokes uniformly.)

Same 4 pinned floors, 35 strokes, 24-lane grid, far_to_near's pose. These are
ORACLES: they read the TRUE grit field to choose each next lane, so they measure
the ceiling of adaptive allocation, i.e. what a perfect policy could aspire to.

  schedule                          clean
  uniform (24 lanes)                0.301 +- 0.050
  greedy (dirtiest lane each time)  0.344 +- 0.034
  sweep once, then greedy           0.307 +- 0.034
  greedy + pose tightened on worn   0.320 +- 0.046
  --
  best uniform (30 lanes, above)    0.347 +- 0.045
  far_to_near                       0.333 +- 0.046

An oracle with perfect knowledge of where the grit is lands at 0.344, which is
the best uniform schedule (0.347) to within noise. Adapting the POSE to the
yield field as well (0.320) does not help either.

THE VALUE OF INFORMATION IN THIS TASK IS ABOUT ZERO. That is a stronger result
than the spacing sweep, and it survives the objection that prompted it.

WHY, mechanically. The floor is nearly homogeneous: load_mean 0.02 kg/m^2 with
load_std 0.009. With 35 strokes and a fixed swath, total area swept is fixed no
matter which lanes are chosen, so "put the next pass where the most grit is"
reduces to "put it where I have not been" -- which is what uniform sweeping
already does. Greedy does show a tighter spread (+-0.034 against +-0.050), so
adapting buys CONSISTENCY across floors, just not a better mean.

CONSEQUENCE FOR ISSUE #8. This cuts against the partially-observed variant more
than anything else measured. The case for #8 was that the agent is handed the
worn-lane map and must instead discover it. But if an oracle holding that map
cannot turn it into a better score, then hiding it does not create a problem --
it creates a harder version of a problem whose solution does not depend on the
hidden information. Making adaptation pay would need a genuinely more
heterogeneous floor, which is a modelling choice about Andrew's shop rather than
a free parameter.
