# The `reach` diagnostic: can a policy gradient move the policy at all?
#
# NOT part of the answer to Andrew's question. It exists to separate two
# explanations for the RL failure that the cleaning task cannot tell apart.

THE AMBIGUITY

  "the learner is broken"  -- the trained policy scored 0.031 against a
                              uniform-random 0.235. Below random is damning.
  "the problem is flat"    -- there is nothing to learn, so the policy never
                              moved from its initialisation.

Both look identical from outside, and the evidence cuts both ways. The critic
reaches EV 1.000, so the network, optimiser and data path all work. But a
freshly initialised network emits a near-CONSTANT action (small weights, output
~ bias ~ 0), and a constant action stands still and drains 0.000 kg -- which is
exactly what the trained policy did. Entropy inflating to 6.2 is also what a
policy does when the gradient carries nothing and only the entropy bonus pushes.
That is a coherent story for "the policy never moved", not for "broken".

THE TEST

Replace the cleaning reward with: walk the wand tip to a target, which respawns
when reached. Progress-based, so it is dense; telescoping to
(start distance - end distance), so circling cannot farm it; target bearing in
the observation, so it is solvable rather than merely hard.

REFERENCES, 600 steps (120 s), 3 seeds, measured BEFORE any training:

  policy       return   targets hit
  oracle       +32.94          27.7      walk straight at it -- the ceiling
  random      -116.09           0.3      uniform actions every step
  constant    -120.00           0.0      what an untrained network emits

153 reward units separate "never moved" from optimal, and random is barely
distinguishable from constant. Contrast the cleaning task, where random scores
0.235 against an optimum of 0.333 -- 70% of the way there for free. That
contrast IS the reason this diagnostic exists.

THE RESULT: PASS, decisively, at update 20

Run `reach-diag` on a 4090, 2026-09-19 (W&B 8nckbota):

  upd  20/457   r -0.10   adv_std 1.5733   H 4.549   6142 steps/s

r = -0.10 against a random baseline of -0.193 and a constant-action -0.200.
Twenty updates was enough to leave the untrained score decisively behind and
head for the oracle's +0.055. The advantage signal tells the same story from
the other side:

  adv_std, reach task      1.5733
  adv_std, cleaning task   0.08 - 0.14   (and collapsing toward zero)

roughly 15x more usable gradient signal, on the same trainer, same network,
same optimiser, same batch size.

SO: THE MACHINERY IS FINE, THE CLEANING TASK IS FLAT. The policy gradient moves
the policy quickly when there is something to move toward. The earlier
below-random score was a policy that never left its initialisation because
nothing was pulling it, not a learner that could not learn. That retires the
"the learner is broken" hypothesis and makes issue #8 -- giving the agent
something it must discover -- the right next step rather than a gamble.

READING THE RESULT

  clearly above -116   the machinery is fine. The cleaning landscape really is
                       flat, and the fix is a better problem (issue #8, the
                       partially-observed variant) rather than a better learner.
  at or near -120      the policy gradient is not moving the policy. Fix that
                       first; no reward design for the cleaning task will help,
                       and the #8 work would be built on sand.

Run it with `scripts/train.py --reach`.
